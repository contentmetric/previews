Custom wardrobes & built-ins

Homes

Built-in wardrobes made for your wall

A built-in wardrobe only works if it fits the room you actually have. Walls lean, ceilings dip and floors slope, especially in older HDB flats. A catalogue wardrobe leaves gaps and filler strips. A custom wardrobe from Midas Interior Solutions is drawn for one wall and cut for one wall.

We are a direct carpentry workshop in Woodlands with our own CNC machine. We measure your room, draw the wardrobe, cut every part in our own workshop and install it ourselves, with no middleman and nothing subcontracted. If you are looking for a built in wardrobe in Singapore that finishes cleanly at the ceiling and the side wall, that is the job we do.

What we make

Every wardrobe is planned around how you store clothes, not around a standard module.

  • Full-height built-in wardrobes: floor to ceiling, with no dust-catching gap on top.

  • Sliding door wardrobes: track doors that save the swing space in small bedrooms.

  • Swing door wardrobes: hinged doors with soft-close hinges and full access to every shelf.

  • Walk-in wardrobes and dressing walls: open shelving, hanging rails, drawers and shoe racks in one run.

  • Corner and alcove wardrobes: units shaped around a bay, a beam or an odd corner.

  • Feature wardrobe walls: arched niches, lit display cabinets, glass fronts and drawer banks, as on our own jobs.

Sliding door wardrobe in Singapore

A sliding door wardrobe is popular in HDB bedrooms and condo master rooms because the doors never swing into the bed or the walkway. It also asks more of the carpenter. The doors run on a top and bottom track, so the opening has to be square and the track has to be dead level.

We check the opening on site in several places and draw the door panels to those readings. A door that is a few millimetres off will catch on the track or leave a visible gap at one side. We also advise on door weight, since tall panels in heavy finishes need a good track. We tell you the trade-offs in plain words: sliding doors open only half the width at a time, while swing doors open the whole wardrobe.

How we make sure it fits

Fit is where most wardrobe problems start. Our answer is to measure properly and to cut only to the drawing.

We measure the width at the top, middle and bottom, and the height at both ends and the centre. If the wall is out of square, we draw the wardrobe with a scribe panel at the side, so the face of the cabinet sits straight and the wall takes up the difference. We note the ceiling level, any coving, skirting, switches, sockets and the position of the air-con trunking.

Parts are cut on our own CNC machine in our own workshop from the signed drawing, so each panel matches the measurement. The same team that measures is the team that cuts and installs, so nothing is lost in a handover to another company.

Materials and Singapore humidity

Singapore humidity typically sits between 70 and 90 per cent. Wood-based boards take up moisture and can swell at the edges or lift their laminate if they are poorly sealed. We pay attention to three things.

First, the board: we choose it for the job and the area, and we explain the difference between plywood and particle board or MDF when you ask. Second, edge banding: every cut edge is sealed so moisture has a harder time getting in. Third, the back panel and the position of the wardrobe: a wardrobe on an outside wall or next to a bathroom needs more care than one on an inner wall.

Finishes are laminate or veneer, chosen with you. Your quote lists the board, the finish and the hardware for your job.

From measurement to installation

Five steps, with the same team throughout.

  1. Send us a photo: you message us the wall on WhatsApp and tell us what you want to store.

  2. Measure on site: we visit, measure, check squareness and level, and agree the layout with you.

  3. Drawing: we draw the wardrobe to scale, with the internal layout and door type, and you confirm it before we cut.

  4. Make: each part is cut to the drawing on our own CNC machine and finished in our own Woodlands workshop.

  5. Install and walk-through: we fit the wardrobe, adjust the doors and drawers, and go through it with you.

What to prepare before we measure

A few things on your side help us get the numbers right.

  • Finish the wet works first: plastering, tiling and painting change wall sizes, so we measure after them.

  • Clear the wall: we need to reach the floor, the ceiling and both side walls.

  • Tell us what you store: long coats, folded knits, suitcases and shoes need different heights.

  • Mark fixed items: air-con units, trunking, power points and the door swing of the room.

  • Share your budget and the finish you like, even as a photo from the internet.

Common questions
What is the difference between a built-in and a custom wardrobe?

A built-in wardrobe is fixed to the room and often runs to the ceiling. A custom wardrobe is made to your measurements. Ours are both: drawn for your wall and installed as part of the room.

Should I choose sliding doors or swing doors?

Sliding doors suit tight bedrooms because they take no swing space. Swing doors give full access to the whole wardrobe at once. We go through both with you when we measure.

When do you measure?

After plastering, tiling and the other wet works are done. Walls change size during those works, so measuring earlier risks a cabinet that does not fit.

Will a wardrobe warp in Singapore humidity?

Moisture is the main risk to any board-based furniture here. We seal the cut edges, choose boards for the room, and tell you which walls need extra care.

Do you offer a warranty?

Your quote states the warranty in writing, after we have measured and agreed the job.

Can you build a wardrobe around a beam or an odd corner?

Yes. We measure the beam and the corner on site, draw the wardrobe around them, and cut the parts on our CNC machine.
