Direct workshop pricing: what it means

Midas is a direct carpentry workshop. The piece you order is drawn by us, cut in our own workshop in Woodlands on our own CNC machine, and installed by our own team. You pay the people who make it.

That matters for the price in a simple way. When carpentry passes through a showroom, a reseller or a subcontractor, each one adds a margin before the quote reaches you. Here there is no middleman and nothing is subcontracted, so the price you see is the workshop's price.

It does not mean every job is cheap. Size, board, finish, hardware and shape still decide most of the cost, as the next section explains. It does mean you are paying for carpentry, with no layer in between, and your quote shows what each line is for.

What sets the price of custom carpentry

Two cabinets of the same width can cost very different amounts. These are the factors that move the price.

  • Size in foot-run: The longer the run of cabinets along the wall, the higher the price. Height matters too, as full-height units use more board.

  • Carcass board: The carcass is the box behind the doors. Standard plywood costs less than moisture-resistant board, which is worth considering for kitchens and bathrooms.

  • Finish: Laminate is the usual starting point. Real wood veneer and sprayed paint finishes cost more because they take more material and more labour.

  • Internal fittings: Drawers, pull-out racks, internal dividers and lighting add to the price.

  • Hardware: Soft-close hinges and runners, handles and sliding tracks vary a lot in quality and cost.

  • Curves and complex shapes: Arches, curved fronts and niches take more design and machine time than straight boxes.

  • Site access: Lift size, floor level, working hours and the distance from the loading bay all affect installation time.

How carpentry is usually priced in Singapore

Cabinets are normally priced per foot run. One foot run is one linear foot of cabinet measured along the wall. A wall of 10 feet with both top and bottom kitchen cabinets is often counted as 20 foot-runs, because the top and bottom cabinets are priced separately.

Wall panels, feature walls and platforms are usually priced per square foot instead. You multiply the height by the width of the panel to get the area.

Always ask what a quoted rate covers. Worktops, premium hardware, upgraded finishes and special board are often priced separately from the base rate.

Kitchen cabinet price in Singapore: what published lists show

Published Singapore carpentry price lists in November 2026 show a wide spread. Kitchen cabinets run from about $125 to $320 per foot run, depending on whether the list counts top and bottom cabinets together or apart. Wardrobes with swing doors run from about $200 to $330 per foot run, and sliding-door wardrobes from about $250 to $400.

TV consoles show about $150 to $320 per foot run, and shoe cabinets about $150 to $300. Feature walls are listed at about $23 to $35 per square foot, and fluted panels at about $50 to $70 per square foot.

The same lists show veneer costing roughly 30 per cent more than laminate and sprayed finishes roughly 45 per cent more. These are market ranges. They are not Midas prices.

Why a range is only a starting point

Price lists assume a standard specification and a straight, simple wall. Your wall may have a beam, a window or a curve. Your chosen finish may be above the standard one.

A very low rate often leaves out something that appears later, such as hardware, installation or board upgrades. Compare quotes line by line, not just by the rate.

How Midas quotes

We quote after we measure. A quote based on a photo can only be a rough guide, because the exact length, height and shape of your space decide the price.

Your quote comes straight from the workshop that will cut and install the piece, with no middleman. It is itemised. It lists each piece, its size, the board, the finish and the hardware, so you can see where the money goes and compare it fairly. The warranty and the payment terms are stated in the quote in writing.

Ways to keep the price down without hurting the fit

Choose laminate for large surfaces and keep veneer or spray for the pieces people see first. Use standard hardware in storage areas and spend on the drawers you open daily. Keep shapes simple where nobody will notice, and use curves where they count.

Finalise your layout before we measure. Changes after drawing and cutting start cost more than changes on paper.

Common questions
How much does carpentry cost in Singapore?

Published price lists show wide ranges, from about $125 to $400 per foot run depending on the item, finish and what is included. Your price depends on your space and choices, so we quote after measuring.

Is a direct workshop cheaper than going through a designer or a showroom?

There is no reseller or subcontractor margin in our price, because we make and install the piece ourselves. The price still depends on size, board, finish and hardware, so compare quotes line by line rather than by the rate alone.

What is a foot run?

One foot run is one linear foot of cabinet measured along the wall. Top and bottom cabinets are usually counted separately.

Is the kitchen cabinet price per foot run or for the whole kitchen?

Most Singapore quotes use a rate per foot run, with the worktop and some hardware priced separately. We show every item in our itemised quote.

Can you give me a price from a photo?

We can give you a rough idea of what to expect. The firm price comes after we measure, because the exact size and shape decide it.

What is usually left out of a carpentry rate?

Countertops, premium hardware, veneer or sprayed finishes and upgraded board are often charged on top. Ask each contractor to list them.
